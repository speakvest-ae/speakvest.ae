02 — Off-plan, explained

In Dubai, your off-plan money is locked by law.

Since 2007, every off-plan project in Dubai has had to keep buyers' money in its own escrow account at an approved bank. Here's what happens to every dirham you pay.

01 / 06

You pay into escrow, not the developer.

Every instalment goes into the project's own escrow account at an approved bank, reported to the Land Department.

Dubai escrow law
02 / 06

One project. One account.

A developer building five towers needs five escrow accounts. Your money can only build your project, never another tower.

One account per project
03 / 06

Creditors can't touch it.

If the developer owes money elsewhere, those creditors can't seize the payments sitting in your project's escrow account.

Ring-fenced funds
04 / 06

Released as the building rises.

Money is paid out in stages against construction progress, checked by independent engineers before each release.

Approved by RERA
05 / 06

A retention is held back.

Even after completion, part of the account (typically 5%) is retained for a year after the units are registered to buyers.

Defects cover
06 / 06

If it stalls: finished or refunded.

If a project is not completed, the escrow bank and the Land Department step in to complete it or refund buyers. Misusing escrow funds is a criminal offence.

Buyer protection
SAFE

Registered, owns the land, escrow first

A developer must be licensed, open the escrow account against the land's title deed, and get approval before advertising off-plan units.

YOUR JOB

Pay only into the escrow account

Escrow only protects money that actually goes into it. Never pay cash, a personal account or a general company account.

5-MINUTE CHECK

Look the project up on Dubai REST

The Land Department's official app shows the project, the developer, completion progress and escrow details.
