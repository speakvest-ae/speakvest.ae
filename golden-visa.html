Eligibility

Who qualifies through property?

  • Value of AED 2 million or more. The value that counts is the one recorded by the Land Department, not a bank valuation.
  • One or several properties. You can usually combine more than one property to reach the threshold.
  • Joint ownership. Co-owners can apply if each person's share meets the threshold (spouses may be treated differently).
  • Mortgaged property can count, usually with a no-objection letter from the bank. Rules on how much must already be paid have changed over time.
  • Off-plan property can count when bought from approved developers, subject to conditions on the amount paid. Confirm the current position before you rely on it.
Smaller budget? Property worth less than AED 2M may still qualify for a shorter investor residence visa. Ask us for the current options.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Buy and register the property

    Make sure the title deed (or Oqood for off-plan) is in your name and shows the value.

  2. Get the property valuation / eligibility confirmation

    In Dubai this is handled through the Land Department's services, which confirm the property meets the investor criteria.

  3. Apply for the Golden Visa

    Apply through the official channels (GDRFA in Dubai, ICP for other emirates), often via a Land Department or Amer service centre.

  4. Medical test and Emirates ID biometrics

    Complete the medical fitness test and fingerprints in the UAE.

  5. Receive your visa and Emirates ID

    Then apply for your family members as your dependants.

Documents you'll typically need

  • Passport copy (valid for at least six months) and a recent photo
  • Title deed or Oqood certificate
  • Bank no-objection letter, if the property is mortgaged
  • Payment receipts or a statement of account for off-plan property
  • Health insurance valid in the UAE

What does it cost?

Expect government application and issuance fees, the medical test, Emirates ID and insurance, plus fees for each family member. Totals vary by emirate, channel and family size, so ask us for an up-to-date estimate before you start.

Rules and fees change. Always confirm current requirements with the Dubai Land Department, GDRFA or ICP before you buy a property for a visa.
